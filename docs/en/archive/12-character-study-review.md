# Character studies and visual acceptance

2026-09-12. This document records native 3D studies made through the official Blender MCP (the `uvx blender-mcp` add-on, `blender-mcp` 1.9.1, a different build from the "Blender Lab" MCP used for v1–v3; see the note in the [v4 evidence](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-study-v4.json)). **Visual acceptance did not pass. The studies do not replace the brand hero image, and they are not the final character.**

## The goal has not changed

The identity reference is still the primary cat in the upper left of the [user concept board](../../source/peipei-concept-board.png). What is needed is a gray-brown tabby with warm white, a natural white inverted-V face marking, a short muzzle, moist hazel-green eyes, a soft fluffy silhouette, and a lying pose with the head and front paws resting together in a relaxed way. The user explicitly asked for it to be "very soothing." A successful tool connection, a mesh count, and a finished render cannot stand in for that judgment.

All four versions are offline Blender Cycles renders of real meshes and hair. No image plane was used to impersonate the cat, and none of this proves a realtime desktop result. Each version is kept as production reference. It is not a reason to lower the quality target. Curve counts only describe the structure of the study. They do not mean quality or performance has been met.

## Specific problems found

1. **The facial anatomy is still unnatural.** Merging ellipsoids and Boolean eye sockets only partly solved the seams and the bulging eyes. The eyelids, forehead, cheeks, and short muzzle still lack a continuous sculpted relationship.
2. **The hair flow is still not natural enough.** v1/v2 particle hair mostly grew outward from the surface. v3 already has different comb directions for the forehead, cheeks, chest, paws, and body, but the clumps, undercoat, and guard hair lack layers, and the ear edges are still unnatural.
3. **The tabby is a procedural stripe experiment.** The forehead stripes, the stripes on both sides of the face, and the back stripes have not been calibrated. A runtime skin should use UVs / masks that can be checked. It cannot depend on each primitive's own coordinates.
4. **The eyes still need a complete structure.** v3 replaced the coarse eye rims with a thin outline, but that is not a complete eyelid, and the eyes still feel like discs. Readability at small size should come from the iris, the cornea, eyelid coverage, and gaze direction together. The spheres and highlights cannot keep being enlarged without limit.
5. **There is no production rig yet.** The v1 body-scale keyframes are only a placeholder for breathing. There is no blink deformation, no quadruped skeleton, no animation blending, and no runtime hair asset.

v4 (the [evidence](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-study-v4.json)) made substantial changes against items 1/2/3/4: the eyes were replaced with a complete structure of sclera + iris + pupil + a double eyelid crease + a baked highlight, and the unequal eye height left over from v2 was fixed; the face marking became a soft gradient; the hair was layered into guard hair + undercoat, the total rose to about 1.16 million, and per-hair color was baked (this removed a coordinate-system bug that had been quietly flattening the tabby in the render). Item 5 moved from "none at all" to "partial": a 24-bone skeleton, automatic-weight skinning on the tail, a jaw bone, and a tongue mesh were added. After the user, in the same round, added a request for a full perceptible range of forms — walk / run / jump / lie down / lie flat, expressions, tongue, and the rise and fall of ears / tail / belly — the same round also closed the biggest gap at the time, **binding the hair to skeletal deformation**. All 36 hair objects were converted into surface-UV-bound curves and checked by rendering two pose sets, a tail swing and front-and-hind leg swings (screenshots are in the evidence file). The hair follows the mesh deformation and no longer detaches. What is still not done well: the Boolean-cut mouth cavity is too shallow and too small, so an open mouth is not visually believable, and the cut itself still leaves a bald patch that has to be covered by a patch mesh; no expression shape keys were made; when leg motion was tried for a standing pose and a crouch, the leg-bone roll axes turned out to be uncalibrated and the paws swung into the face. That was restored to the rest pose and not adopted.

## What should be solved first

Calibrating the leg-bone roll axes was already handled as a follow-up in the same round: `align_roll` was set explicitly on 10 leg bones, and three escalating poses were rendered to verify it (a small angle on one leg → large angles on the front and hind legs → a full extended-leg standing pose). The paws now swing correctly downward / forward and no longer swing into the face. That process also found a new constraint: rotating the hip / spine bones more than a few degrees (measured at −8° / +4°) pulls the eyes, through the Neck → Head → Eye bone chain, off the fixed Boolean-cut eye sockets on the head mesh and exposes a hole. The rig is not broken (the eyes do follow the Head bone). The socket depth can only tolerate a very small range of head rotation. If walk / run need more spine rotation, the sockets have to be cut deeper first, or the spine-rotation budget has to be narrowed.

After the leg bones could be posed, the same round also produced the first real walk cycle: the `LX_v4_WalkCycle` action, 24 frames / 1 second, looping head to tail. The two diagonal pairs, front-left + hind-right and front-right + hind-left, swing in opposite phase. The hind legs bend the knee a bit more during the lift so the paw leaves the ground. Spine swing is deliberately held to 2° (well below the eye-socket exposure threshold found above). Four phases inside the cycle were rendered to verify it: the leg poses really change, the hair follows, and the eyes do not leave the sockets. This is the first time the rig actually carried a motion, rather than only holding a static pose. This walk cycle is a first version made in one pass. Gait naturalness (contact timing, weight shift, ground-contact feedback) was not refined at all. It cannot be treated as finished animation.

After v4, the next priorities are: (1) cut the mouth cavity deeper on the head mesh, and make it follow the jaw's path of motion, or replace the crude Boolean with sculpting, so an open mouth looks believable, and clean up the chin-patch seam at the same time, also cutting the eye sockets a bit deeper to leave room for larger spine motion later (run, jump); (2) add the shape keys the face expressions need (squint, ears flattened, open-mouth panting, and so on, following the six expressions on the user concept board: happy / curious / sleepy / aggrieved / tsundere / acting cute); (3) refine gait naturalness on top of the walk cycle, and make run / jump / lie-down / lie-flat cycles. Until then, hold off on mass-producing more skins and behavior animations, so assets do not accumulate on a look that has not passed acceptance.

Separately, another tool / session in the repository was exploring the same direction at the same time (the 2D action atlas in [`14-daily-life-action-atlas.md`](../14-daily-life-action-atlas.md), and the short-fur references in `assets/characters/lingxi/reference/short-fur/`). In time it overlaps this round of 3D work. The two routes (a 3D long-hair skeleton versus a 2D short-fur atlas) have not converged. Confirm the direction before doing more.

Production acceptance uses the checks below. Aesthetic judgment is not disguised as a precise score:

| Check | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Side by side with the original primary cat, the face marking, face shape, ears, and eyes still look like the same cat |
| Sense of ease | The default lying pose has weight and support, the neck and shoulders are relaxed, and the eyes are not frightened and do not solicit attention |
| Sense of softness | Continuous hair flow and a layered silhouette are visible. No hard brush strokes, smooth plastic surfaces, or harsh seams |
| Free of the set | It still looks natural on a dark desktop, a light desktop, and a transparent background. It does not depend on a photo of a warm room |
| Actual size | At a height of 160–240px the face, the eyes, and the front paws are still recognizable. That size is the prototype check range |
| Motion continuity | Later breathing, blinking, and rest loops do not intercut, slide, or grab attention frequently |
| User response | The user is willing to leave it on the desktop every day. An automated test cannot declare "soothing enough" |

## Reproduction and the verification boundary

In an already running separate Blender official-MCP design session, run these in order:

```sh
.local/blender-mcp-venv/bin/python scripts/blender/mcp_client.py --tool execute_blender_code --code-file scripts/blender/create_cat_study.py
.local/blender-mcp-venv/bin/python scripts/blender/mcp_client.py --tool execute_blender_code --code-file scripts/blender/refine_cat_study.py
.local/blender-mcp-venv/bin/python scripts/blender/mcp_client.py --tool execute_blender_code --code-file scripts/blender/groom_cat_study.py
.local/blender-mcp-venv/bin/python scripts/blender/mcp_client.py --tool render_viewport_to_path --arguments '{"output_path":"anatomy-study-v3.png"}'
```

Reproduce this in a new separate design session. Each script creates a new scene. Calling them repeatedly keeps the old versions and grows the file. The official render tool writes output into Blender's temporary directory, ignores the directory that was passed in, and keeps only the file name. Save the artifact from the returned `filepath`. Do not claim the file was written into the repository based only on the requested path.

Evidence for these calls: [v1 creation](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-study.json), [v1 render](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-render.json), [v2 creation](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-study-v2.json), [v2 face-marking fix](../../evidence/blender-face-mask-fix.json), [v2 render](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-render-v2.json). The final script includes the face-marking fix. Script syntax and engineering tests can verify an executable structure. Character appearance, motion quality, and GPU cost still have to be accepted separately.

Third-version evidence: [creation](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-study-v3.json), [render](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-render-v3.json), [separate background reopen verification](../../evidence/blender-study-reopen.json). After the saved file was reopened, it confirmed 19 mesh objects, 214,819 curve hairs, a valid camera, and transparent render settings. The current character materials have no reference-image nodes. Native curves are not yet bound to surface deformation. You cannot add motion to the body and assume the hair will follow correctly.

The fourth version was executed interactively in the same GUI session through the official `blender-mcp` (`uvx blender-mcp`, a different MCP build from the first three versions; see above and the [evidence file](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-study-v4.json)). The script [polish_cat_study_v4.py](../../../scripts/blender/polish_cat_study_v4.py) is a cleaned-up reproduction of that interactive process, not a line-by-line operation log. During the interactive process a bug occurred twice and was fixed: "a newly created collection / scene with a duplicate name linked objects into the old scene by mistake." The script contains the corrected form. The script also does not include the later exploratory steps that were added on: surface-bound hair, the mouth Boolean, and the patch. That part still lives only in the interactive record. A direct query after saving confirmed: 32 mesh objects, 37 hair-curve objects (36 of them bound to surface deformation), about 1.158 million curves, and 24 bones. To reproduce, run `create_cat_study.py` → `refine_cat_study.py` → `groom_cat_study.py` → `polish_cat_study_v4.py` in order in a new separate session, then do the surface binding by hand (the method is in the changes_from_v3 note in the [evidence file](../../evidence/blender-anatomy-study-v4.json)).
